This week the grandly named https://www.thebrowser.company/[The Browser Company] announced that https://arc.net/[Arc is being sunset] at the end of this year.
Arc has been my go-to browser for three years now, and losing it is a real nuisance.
In the way that trying to tie one's shoelaces with one arm behind one's back is a nuisance.
Arc has become such deeply ingrained muscle-memory for me that I actually need to conciously list out what I need from a replacement with parity of features.

<!-- more -->

As an old fart who remember the browser wars, a choice of IE or Netscape and then being excited when Opera came along as a genuine alternative, the browser landscape today is _very_ different.
It seems there is a pleothera of choice, and sites no longer advertise themselves as "Best viewed with…", so the choice comes down more to the functionality beyond "can it render a page".

TIP: Browsers are a very personal choice, and for how much of _everything_ is done through websites these days, a core tool in how one _surfs the information superhighway_.
So what I'm covering here is how _I_ use it.
You may use it differently, and that's cool. 
Different strokes for different folks. 

## The stuff I use in Arc that I'll miss

### Tab and bookmark management

Arc does away with the idea of Bookmarks and instead stores sites that you want to store over a sidebar as "pinned" tabs.

![](/images/2026/10/SCR-20261025-hvh.png)

Within the tabs on the side, you can put them in folders, and nest those folders.

![](/images/2026/10/SCR-20261025-i05.png)

The difference between this and, for example, Chrome's new suport for vertical tabs is that Arc _only_ has vertical tabs, and they're either pinned (bookmarked) or not.
Other browsers that retain bookmarks then treat tabs as just artefacts along the way, with a mish-mash of bookmarks, tabs (vertical or horizontal), and tab groups.
Pinned tabs get moved out of a tab group, which then destroys the organisation of them.

### Auto Archive / Ephemeral tabs

Not all tabs are equal on the side bar.

![](/images/2026/10/SCR-20261025-i1r.png)

The bookmarked ones are permanent (tagged <1> in the image above).
Ones that you've just opened as you click around randomly trying to find a recipe to cook (<2>) stay open until you decide to bookmark them ("Pin" as Arc calls it), or until they are archived.
Archiving is such a cool feature—after 12 hours (by default) the tab gets closed.
It's not lost, of course - you can retrieve it or search through the archive:

![](/images/2026/10/SCR-20261025-i6i.png)

This feature is great for avoiding tab-sprawl, and a gentle nudge towards the discipline of "do I need this? if so I'll pin it".

### Spaces

Rounding out the bookmark/tab management is Spaces.
This is a great way of partitioning your browsing by task, for example.
Arc has a fancy Spaces page (which I don't use) but you can see here an idea of how I split my browsing by space:

![](images/2026/10/SCR-20261025-iaa.png)

This is one of the muscle memories I use the most - with a hotkey press (Ctrl plus the space number) I easily flit between various different tasks, each nicely isolated from the other.
It's kinda like Chrome's tab groups, but much, much cleaner.

### Split pane

Within the web browsing itself split pane is a killer feature.
Like space-switching, it's hotkey driven. 
Hold down option and click a link, and it opens it _to the side of the current tab_.

This is fantastic for when you're clicking around exploring a site and you're not sure where the link will take you or if it's what you need.
Instead of opening it as a new tab (now you lose the context of your first), or clicking through and then back if it's not right (but now you lose the source page you were on and maybe there were two links you wanted to check out), you retain the context and navigation of where you were whilst exploring additional links.

You can also pin (bookmark) split tabs, like this:

![](/images/2026/10/SCR-20261025-igl.png)

### Copy current URL (and hotkeys for everything in general)

I'm a keyboard kinda guy, and when I'm powering my way around the information superhighway I like to be able to rapidly switch between tabs, spaces, etc from my keyboard.

One shortcut I use in particular is to copy the current URL to clipboard.

Find an interesting site you want to share, hit `Cmd-Shift-C`.

![](/images/2026/10/SCR-20261025-ije.png)

That's all, but the number of times I inadvertently launch Chrome's developer tools when I'm using Chrome (at work) shows me how deep this muscle memory is.
Yes I could move the mouse cursor to the address bar and copy the URL from there…but why would I spend extra precious seconds doing that?

### Sync to other computers and iOS

This is one I'd probably be willing to give up if I had to, but Arc has a signin (which I didn't love at first, but now appreciate) and wherever I use Arc (including on my phone) all my spaces and bookmarks are there.
I can browse on my phone, find a site and pin it, and when I open Arc on my laptop the site is there.

### Chrome extensions

Not many, but I do need things like 1Password, Inoreader, and a few others. 

## The stuff that I really don't care about (and would actively avoid in a replacement browser)

AI.

I _love_ AI for the right things, but browsing, nah.
If I want AI superpowers on the web I'll tell Claude to go do its thing.
I don't want my regular browser to take that role.
I want a browser to browse.
Not summarise, or give me a morning briefing, or any of that other enshittified nonsense (especially since there'll be a cost, either privacy or financial or both).

## Alternatives

NOTE: I'm evaluating these all based on the stock install.
I'm not considering extensions that enable missing features.

### Zen

Zen (https://zen-browser.app/) is an open-source (MPL 2.0) browser based on Firefox.
You can find its repo here: https://github.com/zen-browser/desktop.

[cols="1,1"]
|===
| Ease of import | 👎 No native import from Arc.
Need to go via HTML bookmarks format, one per space.
Imports as bookmarks, not pinned tabs.
| Vertical tabs & bookmarks (pinned tabs) | Yes
| Folders, and Nested folders | Yes
| Auto Archive / Ephemeral tabs | Yes
| Spaces | Yes
| Split pane | Yes, but could't work out how to add a shortcut key so that clicking a link directly opens it in a split (in Arc you just hold the Option key whilst clicking a link to do this).
| Copy current URL shortcut| Yes
| Sync to other computers and iOS | Using Firefox Mobile
|===

#### Notes on Zen

Unlike many other presumptious browsers, Zen defaults to _not_ being the default, which I like 

![](/images/2026/10/SCR-20261025-l61.png)

It's based on Firefox, which makes me nervous - the few times I've enountered Firefox in recent months it's seemed an enshittified crapfest.

But: it looks and feels like…Arc.

There are vertical tabs, split across pins and ephemeral tabs, folders and nested folders:

![](/images/2026/10/975eb0fcf76699658c651a51ff206de75113937fdee2933bfcd79f540c577c26.png)

There are spaces, and even "Boosts" which I didn't need in Arc and probably won't do again here, but this just adds to the look and feel and smell of Arc:

![](/images/2026/10/SCR-20261025-ltp.png)

#### Bookmarks rule the roost in Zen, not pinned tabs.

Unfortunately Zen can't import directly from Arc:

![](/images/2026/10/SCR-20261025-lvo.png)

So I exported from Arc (which gives a Markdown file, H1 for spaces, H2 and onwards for folders within that space) and had Claude split it into a regular HTML bookmarks file per space.
This is where it gets a bit odd though.
After importing bookmarks for a particular Space from Arc:

![](/images/2026/10/SCR-20261026-tkr.png)

They don't show up in the left sidebar, but in the Bookmarks menu:

![](/images/2026/10/SCR-20261026-tlt.png)

This highlights an awkwardness in how Zen handles URLs that you want to save.
Whilst Arc literally did away with bookmarks (you either pinned a tab, or you didn't) Zen retains bookmarks whilst _adding_ pinned tabs.

Zen _also_ has a Bookmarks sidebar:

![](/images/2026/10/SCR-20261026-toh.png)

Unfortunately you can't drag and drop bookmarks from here onto the Spaces pinned tabs.
What you _can_ do is…open every bookmark, one by one, and then select them all and pin them:

![](/images/2026/10/SCR-20261026-tqs.png)

Note that if you're used to Arc's Cmd-D to pin a tab, you'll need to remap it in Zen because that defaults to _bookmarking_ a tab instead.

With the bookmarks at the root level now manually opened and re-pinned, I deleted them (using the Bookmark manager), and then found `Open All in Tabs`:

![](/images/2026/10/SCR-20261026-tte.png)

which makes the job marginally quicker, as they all open at once and you can then put them in a folder:

![](/images/2026/10/SCR-20261026-tuf.png)

Once in a folder, they are then pinned

![](/images/2026/10/SCR-20261026-tvb.png)

This seems like an awful lot of work.
In Arc bookmarks didn't exist, so I just got used to pinned tabs with nested folders to organise them.
But the idea of manually migrating a _lot_ of bookmarks from their nested folders:

![](/images/2026/10/SCR-20261026-twq.png)

into pinned tabs in nested folders in Arc really does not appeal.

So maybe this is chance to declare 'inbox zero' of my pinned tabs (i.e. bookmarks) in Arc, and only migrate those from bookmarks imported in Zen into pinned tabs in Zen.

### Orion

Orion (https://orionbrowser.com/) bills itself as a privacy-first browser, from Kagi.
It supports Safari, Chrome, and Firefox extensions.

There's no registration required to use it.
You can pay for "Orion+" if you want to support it.

[cols="1,1"]
|===
| Ease of import | 😐 Offers to import from Arc when you launch, but doesn't keep folders so dumps the whole list down the side.
Keeps all the spaces.
| Vertical tabs & bookmarks (pinned tabs) | Yes, but also shows them across the top, which seems weird ![](SCR-20261027-tbb.png)
| Folders, and Nested folders | ❌ Only within bookmarks
| Auto Archive / Ephemeral tabs | ❌
| Spaces | ✅ Tab Groups
| Split pane | ❌
| Copy current URL shortcut| ✅
| Sync to other computers and iOS | via iCloud
|===


### Dia

Dia (https://www.diabrowser.com/) is what Arc was abandoned in favour of by the company (https://www.atlassian.com/blog/company-news/atlassian-acquires-the-browser-company[now owned by Atlassian]) behind it.
It bills itself as `More than a browser. A better workday.` which tells you a bit about its mission.

When you set up Dia it auto-connects to apps like Slack, LinkedIn, and GitHub (although it's not made clear how it's auto-connected, since I didn't auth to them).
The connected apps enable it to generate this `Morning brief` slop:

![](SCR-20261027-u2c.png)

Dia defaults to making itself your default browser, and when you uncheck it puts another "Try for 7 days" checkbox, also ticked by default. Ick.

### Vivaldi

https://vivaldi.com[Vivaldi] has https://vivaldi.com/press/releases/vivaldi-is-partnering-with-proton-vpn-to-help-people-break-free-from-big-tech/[a partnership with ProtonVPN] and bills itself as `Powerful Personal Private web browser`.
This is as may be, but gets my spidey-senses tingling.
Who's to say ProtonVPN is the right choice?
Since it's enabled by default, it's now capturing all my browsing traffic; do I trust ProtonVPN?
Why am I having to decide that, when I just wanted a web browser…

It's got a crap-ton of config options—probably great if you love tinkering with setups.
Maybe less so if you just want a browser that is like Arc :)

![](SCR-20261027-ugo.png)

Funnily enough it was this pleotheora of options that meant that I missed some of the features at the first pass because enabling them was squirrled away.

The whole browser gives me early 2000s vibes of "let's ship _everything_, and then add some more on top", vs Chrome's "we're a web browser, we browse the web" (and maybe collect all your data for Google, but that's the price you're paying).

There's also some default bookmarks on the start page that give the vibes of paid listings (booking.com, AliExpress) - perhaps I'm being too cynical though and they're just useful starting points…

### Firefox

I have fond memories of Firefox (and Thunderbird) coming out of the Netscape stable and being a good browser if the only other option was Internet Explorer.
These days there have been changes at Mozilla and I've seen less happy things about the browser.

When you launch it you have to expand a dropdown to untick the option that sends `interaction data` back to Mozilla, which I don't love.
It also tries to take you to "Staff recommended extensions" unless you skip the step, as well as set itself as default browser.

It includes a sidebar AI chatbot, which I have zero interest in using, as well as the default page having 'widgets' with a daily game to play, news headlines, and generally the feeling of…crapware.
