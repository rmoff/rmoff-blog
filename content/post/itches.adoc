My background is databases, SQL, ETL, all that kind of thing. Not coding. Well, maybe a hack (and a dirty one) at best - BBC Basic in the old days, VBA in Excel a long time ago, and more recently even trying to link:/categories/go/[learn Go]. All enough just about to get by, none to seriously trouble anyone for whom this is actually their job. And I was quite content with continuing my next decades as I'd spent my previous ones, thinking of fun software, occasionally finding the time to hack at something trivial, but nothing more.

And then agentic coding came along, and turned my world upside down :)

<!--more-->

I thought I'd share some examples of the _sheer fun_ that I've been having with Claude (and recently, Codex).
Don't worry, I'm not going to bore you with claims of having rewritten Photoshop, solved problems that have eluded mathematicians for decades, or even created an alternative to Apache Kafka written in Brainfuck.
Well, the latter one, just a little bit.

It was in https://pluralistic.net/2026/09/25/other-people/#right-there[an article by Cory Doctorow] that I came across the idea of how AI enables you to 'scratch your own itch'.
In short, instead of having to motivate someone else to create software that you need, or tailor their software to behave in a way that you want, Claude Code and other agentic coding tools give you this ability yourself.

Is it production-ready? No! Is it vibe-coded to heck? Yes! Have I even looked at the source code one? Hell no! Does it fix the problem I had? YES!

These are a mixture of publicly available (usually hosted on GitHub pages or Cloudflare Workers) and private (running on my home server).

== 🗺️ When did I last run pas this specific point?

The idea literally came from wondering this very question when I was out for a run. "I've not been this way for a while…_I wonder when I last ran here_".
Strava is fine for collecting the data and showing you heatmaps of your running patterns (as service personal unfortunately found out). But it can't tell you when you ran past _this_ point.

The app sucks in data from Strava, stores it in DuckDB (because, DuckDB), and gives you a nice interface to click around and find what runs passed through a point. I recently updated it to also plot routes based on a given distance and paths run before.

=== 💬 Organise my social posts and trickle-feed them out to the socials

image::/images/2026/11/itches-social.webp[float=right]

Technically this was not _replacing_ a SaaS, but just avoiding paying for one by keeping the backlog of posts locally.
It hooks up to Raindrop.io where I keep bookmarked sites, and pulls in any with a tag that I've specified. I can then format and amend the post, set an image etc, and then it trickle feeds it into the app that does that actual posting.

=== 🏃🏻‍♂️ Render my trail runs into a cool tile for printing in 3D

image::/images/2026/11/itches-trail-print.webp[float=right]

Another 'avoiding paying' rather than replacing. I'd seen a site do something similar, but it wasn't how I wanted and it wanted a ££ subscription.

I'd rather burn my tokens and create it exactly as I want, than pay some SaaS more than the tokens would cost for something that doesn't quite work as I want it ¯\_(ツ)_/¯

Try it: https://trail-print.rmoff.net

=== 🔗 Handle redirects from short domains

I stopped using Rebrandly a few years ago (probably when I stopped giving conference and meetup talks and needing to give out short URLs).
For some reason, each month I still got emails saying I was running out of clicks on my free plan.
I couldn't see which links (because my plan didn't include analytics) and I couldn't tell if they were real clicks or bots. But the emails annoyed me.

So I gave Claude my Rebrandly API key, it sucked out all the shortlinks and their targets, and built something standalone on a Cloudflare Worker for me.

Links still work, nothing breaks, and I stop getting annoying emails :D

Try it: I mean, it's a short link. You can click this if you really wanna see… https://rmoff.dev/kafka101

== Where _was_ this photo taken?

Waaaay before the days of iPhones, I took photos on just regular digital cameras and even what were then called "camera phones" (because you also got phones without cameras, wtf?!). Being as old as I am, this means I've got a ton of photos with no geo-location data. And being a nerd, that bothers me. So Claude built this app for me that groups photos together by time, and I tell it where they were taken. If I took photos on my phone around that time, it suggests that location for the photos.

I don't know if it's actually any use getting the geo-location data, but it's sure fun af having an excuse to go through my old photos!

== Bloggy fun

image::/images/2026/11/itches-blog-clicks.webp[float=right,role=narrow]

This is a separate itch scratch all together, because my blog is a bit more than an "itch". It was running before Claude got its claws into me, and I did the leg-work of setting up Hugo (and previously Ghost, and previously Wordpress) myself, learning things the hard way. What Claude's done is give me insane webdev powers to properly FAFO. If you're reading this then perhaps you're a regular reader and don't object too much to what I've done to the site.
Some of the really neat things include:

- Complete redesign and new layout. Dark mode. Better search. 404 page. More performant.
- GitHub-style activity heatmap for posts per year
- Hook up to Posthog to (anonymously) track outbound clicks from my link:/categories/interesting-links/[Interesting Links] posts, and then use that data to build chart overall and per post of what people found most interesting
- Add a "🔥 Top picks only" filter to Interesting Links
- FUN: add Lemmings and an old-school hit counter (driven by actual Posthog view data) to each page footer
- Automagically imported and cleaned up posts from my old WordPress blog and former employers
